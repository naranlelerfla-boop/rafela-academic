My Journey

This page documents my evolving academic and field journey in environmental and marine studies. It reflects the progression of my interests, practical experiences, and growing orientation toward research and sustainability.

Early Interest and Academic Foundation

Living within a small island context offers continuous exposure to coastal and marine systems where ecological processes are directly intertwined with social and economic structures. In such environments, the condition of coastal ecosystems plays a critical role in shaping resource availability, livelihood practices, and community resilience. This setting highlights the close relationship between environmental integrity and human well-being in coastal regions.

This context encouraged a way of viewing the ocean beyond its immediate utilitarian value, drawing attention to its ecological complexity and vulnerability. An academic interest emerged around how changes in marine ecosystems can be systematically observed and evaluated through scientific approaches. Formal training in Aquatic Resources Management at Pattimura University provided a foundation for understanding these dynamics from physical and ecological perspectives, supporting a more analytical engagement with marine systems.

Field Experiences and Practical Exposure

Hands-on field activities became a turning point in my academic journey. Through direct engagement with coastal and marine environments, I gained practical exposure to ecosystem observation and basic environmental assessment.

These experiences strengthened my understanding of ecological complexity and reinforced the importance of field-based evidence in environmental research.

Laboratory and Analytical Skills

Complementing fieldwork, I was introduced to basic laboratory practices and analytical approaches relevant to environmental studies. These experiences helped me connect empirical observations with data-driven interpretation.

Current Focus and Future Direction

My current academic direction is shaped by an interest in coastal systems, human–environment interactions, and sustainability-oriented research. I aim to further develop my research skills and contribute to scientific understanding that supports environmental protection and informed decision-making.

Field and Practical Highlights

  • Seagrass ecosystem observation and basic assessment
  • Mangrove field practice and environmental observation
  • Plankton sampling and introductory laboratory handling
  • Basic exposure to environmental data observation and interpretation

These academic and field experiences continue to shape my perspective as an emerging environmental researcher. They motivate my commitment to learning, critical inquiry, and contributing to sustainable approaches in environmental and marine science.
